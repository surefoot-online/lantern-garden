## EPILOGUE: THE RECORD WILL REFLECT

---

*From the testimony record, final statement.*

---

Six months after the last crossing described in this testimony — the crossing where I sat in the Garden for the first time and understood what it was — I am here, before this panel, in this room with its manufactured grey and its laminate table and its two flags I have now spent sufficient time studying to have made my peace with their ambiguity.

The record reflects the following:

The mycorrhizal corridor in Jun's world is viable. The Priya Krishnamurthy culture — derived from Wǎng Liling's fifty-two-year-old preservation matrix — has been deployed at the cedar on the north ridge. The connector seeding is establishing at the projected rate. The beetle-front has held at the temperature threshold that the ecologists needed. The corridor is not complete and will not be complete for several years and may encounter setbacks that the current models do not predict. But the probability distribution has shifted. The recovery scenario is available.

In my world, the Burrard corridor project has a mycorrhizal connectivity component that was not in the original brief and that has been cited in two subsequent planning documents as the methodology that should be applied across the city's major green infrastructure projects. This is the prevention work. This is the knowledge the corridor transferred.

Wǎng Liling died in October, in the care facility in Zhonghe District, New Taipei City. She was eighty-eight years old. She was a mycorrhizal scientist who was twenty years ahead of her field and who spent three years in another world and then spent fifty more years in this one ensuring that what she had learned would be available when someone found the place she had left it. The record should reflect that she got it right.

Tanaka Kenji maintained the preservation site for thirty-four years. He is dead. The record should reflect this too: thirty-four years of walking to a cedar twice a week because a woman he loved had left something there and asked him to keep it safe.

I want to say something about these two people that the testimony's structure did not give me room to say.

Liling came back because she was pregnant, which is the fact. But she also came back because she understood that three years in another world's more advanced version of a problem gave her something to bring back. She brought the matrix. She brought the knowledge. She came back because there was something to come back to and something to bring back, and the crossing ran in both directions and she understood this before I did.

I came back from the Garden six months ago with a map I had started and had not finished. I am still finishing it. The Garden is larger than I can see from any single position inside it. I am learning its edges by walking toward them and noting where they resolve and where they don't. This is the landscape architect's methodology for an unmappable site: you work from what is visible toward what is not, and you mark the limit of the visible clearly, and you return.

I return.

The chair has been patient. I have tried to be worthy of that patience.

CHAIR: Ms. Chen. Is there anything else you wish the record to reflect?

MEI: Yes. One thing.

The corridor is in the ground. Fifty-two years of waiting, and it's in the ground. The forest is talking. In both worlds. Through all the people who were taught to listen.

The record should reflect that.

CHAIR: And your place of residence, for the final entry.

*[Pause.]*

MEI: The crossing itself.

CHAIR: That remains —

MEI: I know.

*[The recording light goes out.]*

---

*The room holds the silence the way certain landscapes hold water — not naturally, but by design.*

*Mei Chen rises. She gathers her materials. She walks past the two flags she has made her peace with. She goes out into whatever day it is.*

*The corridor in Jun's world: the connector seeding is in the ground. The beetle-front is holding at elevation. Priya's culture is establishing in the pattern that the model predicted. Thomas has begun the second acknowledgment — the one that is not for the beginning of the work but for its continuation, which is a different kind of acknowledgment, longer and quieter and requiring no audience beyond the trees.*

*Jun is at the monitoring station, at the map table, with the morning data. The data is what the data is. He reads it with the same attention he has brought to data for three years: looking for what is there, not for what he wants to be there. The two things have been, for the past four months, increasingly similar. He notes this without saying it aloud. Some things you hold.*

*In Vancouver, the Burrard corridor project has passed its six-month review. The mycorrhizal connectivity component is establishing ahead of schedule. Hana Park has cited it in two subsequent briefs. Mei will hear about this in the message that is waiting on her phone when she walks out of the Tribunal building. She will read it on the street, in whatever city this is, in whatever light the afternoon is making.*

*In Taipei, the sea-glass chair in Liling's old room has been moved to the common area — the facility gave the family time, and then the room needed to be prepared for someone else. This is what rooms do. The courtyard maple, in its north-facing position, has produced its autumn leaves. They are not the full color that autumn can produce in better light. But they are present. The tree is doing what it can with the conditions available.*

*The care facility's reception still has fresh gardenias.*

*In the between-place, the lantern-plants are in their November posture: lower, the fruiting structures pendant, the luminescence present as it always is for whoever arrives with sufficient patience to see it. The map of the Garden is incomplete. It will be incomplete for a long time. The work of making it will go on.*

*In Jun's world, a monitoring sensor at the north ridge logs a new connectivity reading at the junction between the established network and the newly deployed matrix. The reading is above threshold. The timestamp is 9:47 a.m.*

*The network is talking.*

---

*End of The Lantern Garden Between Worlds*
